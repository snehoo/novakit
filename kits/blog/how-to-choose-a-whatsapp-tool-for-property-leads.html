How to choose a WhatsApp tool for property leads in India

Why the conversation with a buyer ends up on WhatsApp whether you plan for it or not, what actually breaks when a listing stays stuck in a portal, and a plain checklist for evaluating any tool in this category — not just this one.

Photo: Karan Palan / Pexels

Why does WhatsApp matter so much for property leads in India?

Meta's own stated figure puts WhatsApp at 500 million-plus monthly users in India — the largest single-country user base anywhere it operates. For a buyer, WhatsApp isn't a channel they opt into for real estate specifically; it's already open on their phone, already where they message family, already where a broker's number gets saved once and reused for months. A tool that requires a buyer or an agent to leave that app and go somewhere else — a portal login, a separate CRM, a downloaded app — adds friction at exactly the point where the conversation is moving fastest.

What actually goes wrong when a listing stays stuck in a portal?

The buyer's real questions — carpet area, whether the kitchen is modular, can they see it this weekend — usually get asked over WhatsApp anyway, once they've found the listing through a portal or a broker's network. That means the agent ends up retyping details the portal listing already had, and the portal loses the thread the moment the conversation moves to chat. The listing and the actual conversation live in two different places, and neither one has the full picture of where the deal stands.

What should I actually check before buying a tool like this?

This applies whether you're looking at NovaKit or anything else in the category. Five things are worth checking directly, ideally by testing them rather than taking a sales page's word for it:

01

Does it ask, or does it guess?

Give it a message with a fact missing — a price, an area — and see whether it asks you for it or fills in something plausible-sounding. This is the single most important thing to test, because a tool that guesses will eventually put a wrong number in front of a buyer.

02

Is the compliance check automatic, or is it on you?

Some tools rely on the agent to remember not to promise guaranteed returns; others block that language before it's sent. Ask directly which one you're getting.

03

Does it work in Hindi, or only English?

A lot of the actual conversation with a buyer happens in Hindi or a mix of both. A tool that only understands English text misses a large share of how agents actually work.

04

Does it live inside WhatsApp, or does it need a new app?

Every extra login is a reason to stop using something after the first week. If it's not the same WhatsApp you already have open, ask why not.

05

Is the pricing something you can count?

A cap measured in listings or messages a month is something you can plan around. "Unlimited" usually means there's a limit somewhere that isn't written down yet.

A free trial that doesn't ask for a credit card is a reasonable way to test the first two without any commitment — you'll know within a few messages whether a tool guesses or asks.

Where does NovaKit fit into this?

NovaKit currently covers two of the five checks above directly: it writes a listing from what you send it, and drafts objection replies — both on WhatsApp, both checked against RERA-style rules before anything is sent, both working in Hindi and English. It doesn't yet cover every format or every stage of a deal, and this guide isn't the place to claim it does. Whether it or something else fits depends on which of the checks above matter most for how you actually work day to day.

A note on the numbers in this post. Several stats commonly repeated in real-estate marketing content — specific multipliers for how much faster response times convert — turned up without a traceable primary source when checked, so they're left out here on purpose. The one figure used (WhatsApp's user base in India) is Meta's own reported number.
