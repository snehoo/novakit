What this covers
The highest-leverage Claude AI skills for founders — fundraising, sales, legal, PR, and content. What each does, when to use it, and why it outperforms a raw Claude prompt.

Founders spend a disproportionate amount of time on documents that look like writing tasks but are actually research tasks: investor updates that need to reflect current market sentiment, landing pages that need to know what's converting in your category, press releases that need to land in inboxes journalists are actually reading.

Generic Claude does a reasonable job on all of these if you give it good context. NovaKit founder skills do the research for you — checking what investors are prioritising right now, what landing pages in your vertical are converting, what journalists in your niche are currently covering — before producing a single word of output.

Here's every founder-relevant skill on NovaKit, what it does, and when it's worth using.

1. Sales Landing Page Copy — $19

Your landing page is the highest-leverage piece of writing in your stack. It's the first thing a prospect reads after clicking an ad, a tweet, or a Product Hunt listing — and most founder-written landing pages leave significant conversion on the table because they describe the product rather than selling the outcome.

The Sales Landing Page Copy skill researches what landing pages in your specific category are converting before it writes. It checks what headlines are performing, what value propositions are resonating, what objections are showing up in the fold, and what CTA structures are getting clicks in your vertical. Then it builds a complete page: above-the-fold headline and subhead, three-section body (problem / solution / proof), objection-handling block, and CTA. The output is structured for a developer to drop into a page template directly.

Best for: Pre-launch founders validating messaging, post-launch founders whose pages aren't converting, agencies writing landing pages for clients.

2. Pitch Deck Outline — $9

Most AI pitch deck tools produce a slide list. The Pitch Deck Outline skill produces a narrative — because investors don't fund slide lists, they fund stories they can retell in a partner meeting.

Before building your structure, the skill researches what investors in your stage and vertical are currently asking about: what market signals they want to see, what competitive positioning is credible versus overreaching, what traction metrics they're using as proxies for product-market fit at your stage. It asks about your vertical, funding stage, and target investor type (generalist vs. thesis-driven), then builds a 10–12 slide arc with slide titles, the key claim for each slide, and the supporting evidence structure for each claim.

Best for: Pre-seed and seed-stage founders preparing for their first institutional raise, founders pivoting and rebuilding their narrative, accelerator cohort members preparing demo day decks.

3. Investor Update — $9

The monthly investor update is one of the most underused tools in a founder's relationship stack. A good update builds trust, surfaces warm intros, and keeps investors engaged between rounds. A bad one reads like a school report — metrics, narrative, ask — and gets skimmed.

The Investor Update skill builds updates in the format that experienced angels and institutional investors actually prefer: the key metric front and centre, the one thing that went wrong and what you learned, the one thing that went unexpectedly well, the specific ask. It researches what investors in your stage are currently tracking as leading indicators — ARR growth rate at seed vs. Series A looks different — and calibrates your update's emphasis accordingly.

Best for: Post-seed founders with active angel networks or institutional investors, founders preparing for a follow-on raise and building 3–6 month update history.

4. PR Press Release — $9

Press releases written as product announcements don't get coverage. Journalists cover stories — conflict, change, stakes — and a launch announcement has none of those unless the writer builds them in.

The PR Press Release skill researches what journalists in your specific niche are currently covering before writing. It identifies the story angle that fits your announcement to the current editorial conversation in your vertical, writes the opening with the narrative hook first (not the company name and date), and structures the quotes and supporting data to give a journalist what they need to file quickly. A SaaS launch in the HR tech space gets a completely different release than a DTC product launch, because the journalist pool and story frames are different.

Best for: Founders handling their own PR, marketing directors without an agency, companies launching into competitive or noisy verticals where angle matters.

6. Grant & Proposal Writing — $9

Best for: Startups pursuing non-dilutive funding (SBIR, STTR, state programmes), non-profit founders, founders in regulated sectors (health, climate, education) where grant funding is common.

8. Conference Abstract — $9
